## 1. A planning tool, not a survey

**Land Terrain is a planning tool. It is not a land survey, not an engineering design and not a professional opinion.** Contours, flow paths, watersheds, ponding spots, pond surfaces and volumes, driveway grades, slope classes, peak flows and pipe sizes are estimates computed from public elevation and rainfall data. They are meant to help you understand your land and to prepare questions for the professionals who will design and build.

Before you build a dam, pond, road, driveway, crossing, culvert or structure, or make any decision about safety, property lines, permits or money, verify the numbers on site and with a licensed professional (a surveyor or engineer, your county, or your conservation district). Dams and work in streams or wetlands may need permits where you live.

The app does not show property lines. A boundary you draw or import is shown as you gave it.

## 2. The data and its limits

**The app covers land in the United States only.** You may use it from any country, but it cannot add or analyse land elsewhere, with or without Land Terrain Pro.

The app uses public data: the USGS 3D Elevation Program (lidar elevation), the USGS National Hydrography Dataset (mapped streams) and NOAA Atlas 14 (rainfall). These datasets have their own accuracy, age and gaps. Lidar shows the ground on the day it was flown; earthwork, culverts, tile drains, buildings and vegetation can make the real ground and the real flow of water differ from what the data shows.

Every result says what it came from: the resolution, the survey year where known, and the date you downloaded it. Watersheds carry a confidence grade; a low grade means the terrain is too flat or too altered to trust the result. Creek crossing figures are planning estimates. The developer does not control these services and does not guarantee that they are available, current or correct.
